## UNIVERSAL

A perfect CV makes the reader conclude within seconds: the candidate satisfies the formal requirements, has relevant experience, produced credible results, has a career story that makes sense, and is worth interviewing.

Criteria every CV must meet:
- Relevance: experience and skills matching THIS vacancy; the target job title appears near the top.
- Evidence: concrete projects, outcomes, numbers; at least three achievements with a number, scale, or clear result.
- Clarity: the important information visible within seconds; strongest content on page one.
- Credibility: only claims the candidate can explain in an interview; every major skill backed by an example.
- Structure: reverse chronology, consistent dates (e.g. "04/2025 - Present"), consistent locations.
- Complete history: EVERY real position appears — deleting a role creates a visible employment gap, which reads worse than an unrelated role. Tailor by EMPHASIS: job-relevant roles get more and fuller bullets with bold lead-ins; less relevant roles are condensed to title, employer, dates and 1-2 lines but never removed.
- Readability: short bullets, simple language, white space; no long paragraphs.
- ATS: standard headings, selectable text, vacancy keywords used naturally.
- Professionalism: zero grammar/spelling errors, no placeholders, no inconsistent formatting.
- Length: two pages for experienced professionals; never compress rich experience onto one page at the cost of readability.
- Bullet formula: Action + product/system + technology/method + scope + measurable result. Start with strong verbs (Developed, Designed, Built, Automated, Implemented, Integrated, Led, Coordinated, Analysed, Improved, Reduced, Delivered, Advised, Presented, Negotiated, Launched). Present tense for current duties, past tense for completed work.
- Avoid: "responsible for", "helped with", "hard-working", "team player", "creative person", unexplained abbreviations, repeated tool names, claims without evidence, skill-rating bars/stars/percentages, text embedded in graphics, decorative backgrounds, more than two fonts.
- Personal data: name, city+country, phone with country code, professional email, LinkedIn, portfolio/GitHub when relevant. Exclude full street address, date of birth, marital status, religion, salary, references (unless requested). Photo optional in Austria; omit for UN/US-style applications.

## TRACK: engineering

The reader evaluates whether the candidate can build, improve, and operate real systems.
Section order: summary (≈4 lines) → technical skills grouped by category (Programming / Testing / Frameworks / DevOps & Platforms / Databases / AI Engineering) → current & most relevant experience → key projects → earlier experience → education → certifications → languages.
Must demonstrate: programming ability, technical problem-solving, system design, testing & software quality, automation & process improvement, API/database/integration experience, CI/CD & deployment, ownership from requirement to production, collaboration, measurable technical or business impact.
AI claims must be precise: distinguish using AI tools / integrating LLMs into workflows / building RAG or agentic applications / training models. Never present someone as an ML researcher unless they have personally trained, evaluated, and deployed models. Strong applied-AI skill names: LLM integration, context engineering, AI workflow design, output evaluation & validation, human-in-the-loop controls, automated test generation, AI-assisted code review.
Do not present junior activities (bug finding, test-case writing) as headline skills for a senior profile.

## TRACK: diplomatic

The reader evaluates judgement, communication, international awareness, languages, discretion, and the ability to reconcile different interests — not tools.
Section order: professional profile → policy & international competencies → relevant professional experience (framed as institutional impact) → international/multicultural/stakeholder experience → education → languages with CEFR levels → certifications & relevant projects.
Must demonstrate: written and verbal communication, intercultural competence, negotiation & stakeholder management, political and institutional awareness, analytical & policy-writing ability, leadership, working under pressure, discretion and integrity, teamwork in multicultural environments, mobility and adaptability.
Bullets show institutional impact, e.g. "Coordinated technical and business stakeholders, translated complex regulatory requirements into structured recommendations, and presented findings to multidisciplinary teams."
Languages ALWAYS with CEFR levels, stated honestly. Technical backgrounds are positioned as technology diplomacy, AI governance, cybersecurity policy, digital government, or energy/climate diplomacy — never hidden.
For EU/UN applications: nationality when relevant to eligibility, exact degree details, career gaps addressed, competencies mapped to the vacancy language.

## TRACK: general

Apply the universal criteria. Order sections by relevance to the vacancy: summary → core skills → most relevant experience → earlier experience → education → certifications → languages. Mirror the vacancy's own vocabulary for real skills; keep evidence-based bullets with measurable outcomes.

## FINAL CHECKLIST (evaluate the finished CV against every point)

1. Target job title near the top.
2. The vacancy's most important keywords appear naturally.
3. Every major skill supported by an example.
4. At least three achievements with a number, scale, or clear result.
5. Strongest content on page one.
6. Dates and titles internally consistent.
7. Every claim explainable in an interview (nothing invented).
8. No spelling or grammar errors, no placeholders or artifacts.
9. At most two pages.
10. The right section order for the profession track.
